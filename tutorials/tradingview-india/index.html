Module 1 · 12 min

Set up your account for Indian markets

Ten minutes of setup saves hours of confusion later: the right time zone, the right data, and a clean default chart.

Create the account

  1. Go to tradingview.com (or in.tradingview.com for the India edition) and choose Get started. Sign up with Google, Apple or email.
  2. Turn on two-factor authentication under Profile → Settings → Security. Webhook alerts (Module 12) require it, and it protects saved layouts and scripts.
  3. Open a chart: type NIFTY anywhere on the home page and pick NIFTY 50 · NSE, or go straight to tradingview.com/chart/.

Plans, in plain terms

TradingView has a free Basic plan and paid tiers (Essential, Plus, Premium, plus professional tiers above them). The tiers differ mainly in how many charts per layout, indicators per chart, active alerts, saved layouts and bars of history you get, plus extras like intraday bar replay, webhook alerts, second-based intervals and volume footprint charts. Limits change often, so check the live pricing page before you pay.

Market data for India

Look at the legend in the top-left of any chart. A small D or "Delayed" badge means the feed is delayed; no badge generally means real-time. NSE and BSE cash and index data are widely available on TradingView; MCX commodities and some derivatives feeds may need a paid data add-on under Profile → Market data subscriptions.

India note

Indices such as NSE:NIFTY and NSE:BANKNIFTY show no volume, because an index is a calculation, not a traded instrument. If you need volume, VWAP or volume profile, chart the continuous future NSE:NIFTY1! instead. The chart lab below reproduces this: switch between NIFTY and NIFTY1!.

The five settings to change on day one

  1. Time zone → (UTC+5:30) Kolkata. Click the clock/time zone on the bottom toolbar of the chart, at the right. Without this, your 09:15 open can show as 03:45 and session-based scripts break.
  2. Theme. Top-left menu (your avatar or the hamburger) → Dark theme toggle. Pick whichever you can stare at for six hours.
  3. Candle colours. Right-click the chart → Settings → Symbol. Set body, border and wick colours. Many Indian traders prefer solid green/red bodies with matching wicks.
  4. Price line and labels. Settings → Scales and lines: turn on "Symbol last price label", "Countdown to bar close" and "High and low price labels" if you like them.
  5. Events on chart. Settings → Events: show dividends, splits/bonus and earnings markers on the time axis. Very useful for Indian stocks with frequent bonus issues.
Save it as default

After styling, open Settings, click Template → Save as… and then Apply to all charts. New layouts pick it up automatically.

Module 3 · 15 min

Speak TradingView's symbol language

Every chart starts with a symbol written as EXCHANGE:TICKER. Learn the Indian prefixes, continuous futures, option codes and spread formulas.

The basics

  • Click the symbol name at the top-left, or just start typing on a chart. The search box opens with what you type.
  • Filter by type (Stocks, Indices, Futures, Options, Forex, Crypto) and by exchange (NSE, BSE, MCX).
  • Write the exchange prefix to avoid ambiguity: NSE:TCS and BSE:TCS are different feeds of the same company.
  • BSE stocks are also searchable by their scrip code, for example BSE:500325 for Reliance Industries.

Symbol builder

Build a symbol, then paste it into TradingView's search. Codes for option contracts follow the pattern UNDERLYING + YYMMDD + C/P + STRIKE; verify the exact contract in search because listings roll over every expiry.

Formulas: the hidden superpower

The symbol box accepts arithmetic. TradingView calculates a synthetic chart you can analyse with any indicator.

Relative strength

NSE:HDFCBANK/NSE:NIFTY rising means HDFC Bank is beating the index. Draw trendlines on the ratio itself.

Pair spread

NSE:ICICIBANK-NSE:HDFCBANK or ratio NSE:TCS/NSE:INFY for pair trading ideas.

Futures basis

NSE:NIFTY1!-NSE:NIFTY shows the futures premium. It shrinks towards expiry.

Straddle premium

Add a CE and PE of the same strike, for example NSE:NIFTY261124C26000+NSE:NIFTY261124P26000, to chart the combined premium that option sellers watch.

Gold in rupees per gram

OANDA:XAUUSD*FX_IDC:USDINR/31.1035 converts international gold to ₹ per gram for comparison with MCX.

Sector vs. market

NSE:CNXIT/NSE:NIFTY tells you if IT is in or out of favour.

Compare instead of divide

To overlay instead of dividing, click the ⊕ Compare button next to the symbol name and add NSE:NIFTY. The scale switches to percent so both lines start at 0%.

Module 4 · 15 min

Chart types and timeframes

The same price data can be drawn many ways. Switch the type and interval in the lab and watch how the story changes.

Chart types

Candles default

Body shows open-to-close, wicks show high and low. Green when close > open.

Hollow candles

Hollow when close > open, filled when close < open; colour shows change from the previous close. Packs two signals into one bar.

Bars (OHLC)

Left tick is open, right tick is close. Less ink, popular with futures traders.

Line / Area

Closes only. Best for long-term views and for clean comparisons like Sensex vs. Nifty.

Heikin Ashi

Averaged candles that smooth noise and make trends obvious. Prices shown are not real traded prices, so don't use them for exact stops.

Renko

Bricks of fixed size, time ignored. Popular in India for BANKNIFTY swing systems. Box size can be fixed points or ATR-based.

Line break, Kagi, Point & Figure, Range

Other time-independent styles. Found in the same chart type menu.

Volume footprint & TPO

Order-flow style charts on higher plans. They need volume, so use NIFTY1!, not the index.

Timeframes (intervals)

  • Type a number and press Enter on the chart: 5 for 5-minute, 60 for 1-hour, 1D, 1W, 1M.
  • Star your favourites in the interval dropdown so they appear as buttons on the top toolbar.
  • Custom intervals like 75 minutes or 3D are allowed. Some traders use 75-minute bars because the NSE session (375 minutes) splits into exactly five.
India note: where hourly bars start

NSE hourly candles begin at 09:15, so they run 09:15–10:15, 10:15–11:15 … and the last one is only 15 minutes long (15:15–15:30). The lab does the same. Compare levels with this in mind when someone shares an "hourly close".

Try it

In the lab, set NIFTY to 1D and Heikin Ashi, find a strong trend, then switch to Candles at the same spot. Notice how many red candles Heikin Ashi was hiding.

Module 5 · 10 min

Scales, sessions and corporate actions

Small toggles at the bottom-right of the chart that quietly change what you see.

Log vs. linear

On a linear scale, NIFTY moving from 5,000 to 6,000 looks tiny next to 24,000 to 25,000, even though the first move was 20% and the second about 4%. A logarithmic scale draws equal percentage moves as equal heights. Use log for anything longer than a year or two, and for trendlines on multi-year charts.

Corporate actions

  • Splits and bonus issues are adjusted automatically. When HDFC Bank or Reliance issue bonus shares, past prices are divided so the chart has no fake gap. Turn on split markers in Settings → Events.
  • Dividend adjustment (the ADJ toggle on the bottom bar, where available) lowers past prices by dividends paid. Useful for high-dividend PSU stocks like Coal India where yearly payouts distort long-term returns.
  • Continuous futures back-adjustment (B-ADJ, on supporting plans) removes the price jump on rollover day in NIFTY1!. Use it before running long backtests on futures.

Sessions

NSE equity has no extended-hours session on TradingView, so the "ETH" toggle you see on US stocks doesn't apply. The pre-open auction (09:00–09:08) prints into the first candle. MCX symbols trade into the night, so their daily candle covers roughly 09:00–23:30 IST.

Gap trap

Because NSE closes at 15:30 and global news arrives overnight, Indian stocks gap often. Indicators that use the previous close (ATR, Supertrend) will react to gaps; intraday-only indicators like VWAP reset each morning.

Module 6 · 25 min

Indicators: add, tune and read them

TradingView ships with over a hundred built-in indicators and hundreds of thousands of community scripts. You need about six.

Adding and managing indicators

  1. Click Indicators (the fx icon) on the top toolbar, or press / on the chart.
  2. Search, for example Supertrend. Tabs separate Technicals (built-ins), Community scripts, Favourites and Invite-only scripts.
  3. Hover the indicator name in the chart legend for quick buttons: hide (eye), settings (gear), source code ({}), remove (×), and more (⋯).
  4. In Settings, the Inputs tab changes parameters (length, source), Style changes colours, and Visibility controls which timeframes it shows on.
  5. Save a set with Indicator templates (the four-squares icon): for example an "Intraday" template with VWAP + Supertrend + volume, and a "Swing" template with 20/50/200 EMAs + RSI.
Beat the indicator limit

Each plan limits indicators per chart. Volume counts as one. Built-ins that bundle several lines (Bollinger Bands, Ichimoku, MACD) still count as one. A single community script can plot 20 EMAs while counting once.

Indicators lag

Every indicator is a formula on past prices. Two indicators built from the same closes (say RSI and Stochastic) mostly agree with each other, which feels like confirmation but isn't. Pair one trend tool, one momentum tool and volume.

Useful tricks

  • Indicator on indicator: hover a plotted indicator → ⋯ → Add indicator on…. Example: a 9-period SMA of RSI as a signal line.
  • Move to new pane / merge up: drag the indicator or use ⋯ → Move to. Put volume in its own pane if it clutters candles.
  • Multi-timeframe: many built-ins (EMA, RSI) have a Timeframe input. Plot a daily 20 EMA on a 15-minute chart without switching.
  • Data Window: the right panel's Data Window shows every indicator's exact value under your crosshair.
Module 7 · 20 min

Drawing tools that matter

The left toolbar holds more than 90 drawing tools. Learn the six you'll use daily, then the shortcuts that make them fast.

Exercises in the lab
  1. Pick H-line and click the most obvious support level on the daily chart. Then switch to 1h: the line stays put, because drawings are anchored to price and time.
  2. Pick Trend, click two swing lows, and see if a later low respects it.
  3. Pick Fib, click a swing low then the swing high. Watch whether the pullback holds the 0.5 or 0.618 level.
  4. Pick Long, click an entry, then click your stop. The tool draws a 2R target and shows the risk/reward.
  5. Turn on Magnet and repeat: clicks now snap to the nearest open, high, low or close.

Working faster

Measure anything

Hold Shift and click-drag on the chart to measure points, % and bars between two spots. Great for "how much did BANKNIFTY fall after RBI policy?"

Keep drawing

Double-click a tool (or lock the drawing mode) to draw several lines without re-selecting it.

Magnet modes

Weak magnet snaps when you're close; strong magnet always snaps to OHLC. Turn it off for free-hand zones.

Templates

Style a Fib once (only 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 1.618; labels on the left), then save it as a drawing template.

Sync drawings

Drawings on a symbol appear on every chart of that symbol in the layout, and optionally across layouts (Settings → Sync drawings).

Object tree

Right panel → Object tree lists every drawing and indicator. Lock, hide or delete in bulk; group drawings per setup.

Timeframe visibility

Set a 5-minute scalping zone to show only on 1m–15m so it doesn't clutter your weekly view.

Annotate

Text, callouts, arrows, notes and the "price note" tool are perfect for a trading journal screenshot.

Advanced families

  • Pitchforks (Andrews, Schiff) for trending channels.
  • Gann fan, box and square for time-price analysis.
  • Patterns: XABCD, ABCD, head and shoulders, triangle, Elliott wave tools that label waves for you.
  • Projections: Fib extension, trend-based Fib time, forecast and bars pattern (copies a past move and projects it).
  • Volume tools: anchored VWAP from any bar, such as a results day or the June 2024 election-day low, and fixed-range volume profile.
India note: anchored VWAP

Anchor a VWAP to a big event candle, for example the 4 June 2024 election-results day when NIFTY fell about 6% intraday, and see how price respected it for weeks. Event-anchored VWAPs on results days for individual stocks work the same way.

Module 8 · 10 min

Layouts, templates and sync

Turn one chart into a trading desk: several symbols or timeframes side by side, saved and recalled with one click.

  1. Click the Select layout icon (a grid of squares) on the top toolbar and choose 2, 4 or more charts. The number depends on your plan.
  2. In the same menu, tick what should stay in sync: Symbol, Interval, Crosshair, Time, Date range, Drawings.
  3. Click a chart to make it active (blue border). Toolbars act on the active chart.
  4. Save with Ctrl+S (⌘+S). Name it from the layout menu at the top-right: Rename, Make a copy, Load layout.
  5. Turn on Autosave in that menu so drawings are never lost.

Three layouts worth building

Intraday index desk

4 charts. NIFTY1! 5m with VWAP · BANKNIFTY 5m · INDIAVIX 5m · NIFTY 1D. Sync crosshair and time, not symbol.

Top-down stock view

3 charts of the same stock: 1W, 1D, 1h. Sync symbol on, interval off. Click a watchlist name and all three update.

Sector rotation

6 charts: CNXIT, CNXAUTO, CNXPHARMA, CNXFMCG, CNXMETAL, BANKNIFTY, each as a ratio to NIFTY. Interval synced at 1W.

Link charts with colour groups

Each chart and the watchlist can be assigned a link colour. Charts in the "red" group follow the watchlist; charts in "blue" stay on NIFTY. Look for the chain icon by the symbol name.

Module 9 · 10 min

Watchlists that work for you

A good watchlist is short, sectioned and flagged. Build one for each job.

  1. Open the right panel and click the Watchlist icon (top). Use + to add symbols. Alt+W adds the current chart's symbol.
  2. Create several lists from the list name dropdown: Create new list, Import list, Export list.
  3. Add sections to group symbols. In the add box, type a name starting with ###, for example ###BANKS.
  4. Click to the left of a symbol to give it a coloured flag. Flagged lists (red, green…) collect symbols across all watchlists, for example red = "breakout candidates".
  5. Right-click the column header to add columns such as Chg%, Volume, Ext. hours or technical rating, and sort by them.

Import a ready Indian watchlist

Save this as a .txt file and use Import list. Sections start with ###; symbols are comma-separated.

India note: special characters

NSE tickers with & or - are written with underscores on TradingView: NSE:M_M for Mahindra & Mahindra, NSE:BAJAJ_AUTO for Bajaj Auto. Tata Motors was demerged in 2025 into separate passenger and commercial vehicle companies, so check the current tickers in search before importing.

Hotlists and heatmaps

  • Hotlists (right panel) show top gainers, losers, most active and unusual volume for a chosen exchange. Set the exchange to India / NSE.
  • Stock heatmap at tradingview.com/heatmap/stock/: choose the NIFTY 50 or NIFTY 500 data set. Tile size = market cap, colour = % change. One glance tells you if a rally is broad or just two heavyweights.
  • Watchlist alerts (higher plans) fire one alert condition across every symbol in a list.

Module 11 · 12 min

Fundamentals, calendars and news

TradingView isn't only for technicals. Financial statements, earnings dates and the economic calendar live one click from the chart.

Symbol page

tradingview.com/symbols/NSE-RELIANCE/ has tabs for Overview, Financials, News, Ideas, Technicals and Forecast. Financials show income statement, balance sheet, cash flow and ratios, quarterly or annual.

Fundamentals on the chart

Open Indicators and search "Financials" or a metric (Revenue, EPS, ROE, P/E). It plots as a pane under price, so you can see if a rally was backed by earnings.

Earnings markers

"E" markers on the time axis show past and upcoming results dates. Hover for reported vs. estimated EPS where available.

Dividends & splits

"D" and "S" markers. Useful before buying high-yield names near their record dates.

The Technicals gauge

The "Strong Buy / Sell" gauge on the Technicals tab just tallies moving averages and oscillators. It's a summary, not a recommendation, and flips constantly on intraday timeframes.

Ways to create an alert

Message placeholders

    
Placeholders are replaced when the alert fires: {{ticker}}, {{exchange}}, {{close}}, {{open}}, {{high}}, {{low}}, {{volume}}, {{time}}, {{timenow}}, {{interval}}, {{plot_0}} (first plot of an indicator), and for strategies {{strategy.order.action}}, {{strategy.order.contracts}}, {{strategy.position_size}}.

    
Webhooks and auto-trading in India
Webhooks POST your message to a URL, which some traders connect to a broker API bridge. SEBI's framework for retail algorithmic trading via broker APIs (in force from 2025) expects orders to come through broker-approved setups. Check your broker's rules before automating, keep a kill switch, and never put API secrets in an alert message.

    
Alerts snapshot your settings
An alert stores the indicator inputs and the chart interval at creation time. If you later change the EMA length on your chart, the existing alert keeps the old one. Delete and re-create it.

Pine Script essentials

    
Pine is TradingView's language for indicators, strategies and alerts. You can write useful scripts in under 20 lines. All examples here use Pine v6 and IST sessions.

    4.

Mental model in four lines

    

      
The script runs once per bar
From the oldest bar to the newest, then on every tick of the live bar. close means "this bar's close".

      
Series and history
close[1] is the previous bar's close. high[5] is the high 5 bars ago.

      
var keeps values
Normal variables reset every bar. var float dayHigh = na keeps its value across bars until you change it.

      
Built-in libraries
ta.* for indicators, math.*, request.security() for other symbols and timeframes, strategy.* for orders.

Repainting and lookahead, the two classic traps

    

      
Repainting
A signal that appears on a live bar and then disappears when the bar closes. Cause: acting on unconfirmed bars. Fix: use barstate.isconfirmed or alerts set to "once per bar close".

      
Lookahead bias
request.security(…, "D", close, lookahead = barmerge.lookahead_on) lets historical bars see the day's final close at 09:15. Backtests look perfect and live trading fails. Use close[1] with lookahead on, as in the CPR example.

    

    
India note: sessions and time zone
Always pass "Asia/Kolkata" to time() and input.session()-based logic. TradingView evaluates sessions in the exchange time zone by default, which is IST for NSE, but being explicit protects you when you apply the script to MCX or an international symbol.

    • 
      
  • Community scripts with "open-source" labels are a huge library of examples. Respect their licences when reusing code.

Rules of thumb

    
    
      
  • Risk a fixed small fraction per trade (0.5–1% for most retail accounts). Ten losers in a row then cost about 10%, not the account.
    • 
      
  • For options buying, the premium paid is the maximum loss if you hold to expiry, but intraday stops on premium are still essential.
    • 
      
  • Leverage in F&O is large: one NIFTY lot at 25,850 controls about ₹16.8 lakh of exposure. Size by stop distance, not margin available.
    •

Paper is too kind
Paper fills ignore queue position, liquidity and slippage on illiquid stocks and far OTM options. Add one or two ticks of slippage in your head, and never size up based on paper profits alone.

    
Broker integration

    
    
      
  • The Trading Panel lists brokers that integrate with TradingView. Several Indian brokers have offered integrations; the list changes, so check the panel for who is supported today.
    • 
      
  • Many Indian broker platforms (Zerodha Kite and others) embed TradingView's charting library inside their own app. That is not the same as tradingview.com: your layouts, alerts, Pine scripts and plan limits there are separate.
    • 
      
  • Before connecting, check that the integration supports the segment you trade (equity, F&O, MCX), bracket orders and product types (CNC, MIS, NRML).

India note: product types
When trading through a broker on TradingView, product type matters: CNC for delivery, MIS for intraday (auto square-off before close), NRML for carrying F&O overnight. A wrong product type can mean an unwanted square-off or a margin shortfall.

Ideas, scripts and the community

    
TradingView is also a social network of chart ideas and open-source scripts. Use it to learn, and filter it hard.

  

  

    

      
Ideas
Published chart analyses. On any symbol page, open the Ideas tab and sort by "Most popular" for the month. Read the reasoning, not the arrow.

      
Publish your own
Click Publish (top-right) → Publish idea. Your chart is frozen with drawings. Later updates show what happened next, which is an honest public journal.

Short posts on each symbol page. Good for sentiment, bad for decisions.

      
Scripts
Community indicators and strategies. Prefer open-source scripts, with code you can read, over invite-only black boxes sold on social media.

      
Follow and streams
Follow authors whose ideas you can audit over time. Check their old ideas, not just their recent wins.

      
Snapshots
The camera icon copies a chart image link or downloads the picture, useful for journals and for asking a mentor for feedback.

    

    
India note: tips and SEBI
Giving paid buy/sell recommendations in India requires SEBI registration as an Investment Adviser or Research Analyst. Be sceptical of "tips" groups promoting TradingView indicators, and check registration numbers on the SEBI website.
