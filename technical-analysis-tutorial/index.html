01 Foundations

What technical analysis is, and what it isn't

Technical analysis (TA) studies price, volume and positioning data to judge the probable direction of a market and, more usefully, where you are wrong.

A fundamental analyst asks what is this business worth? A technical analyst asks what are buyers and sellers doing right now, and where does that behaviour change? Both can be right at the same time. HDFC Bank can be a fine business while its stock goes sideways for three years; a small-cap can be overvalued and still double on momentum.

TA rests on three working assumptions, first set out by Charles Dow over a century ago:

  1. Price discounts what is known. Results, FII flows, RBI policy expectations and rumours all show up in the order book before they show up in the newspaper.
  2. Prices move in trends. A trend in motion is more likely to continue than to reverse. Most TA tools are ways to spot, ride or exit trends.
  3. Behaviour repeats. Fear and greed produce recognisable shapes, and traders remember levels where they lost or made money.

What TA cannot do: predict the future with certainty, protect you from overnight news (a Hindenburg report, a surprise budget tax change, a lower circuit), or turn a bad risk process into a good one. Treat every chart reading as a probability with a defined exit, never as a forecast.

Investors

Use weekly and monthly charts to time entries into stocks they already like on fundamentals, and to avoid catching falling knives.

Swing traders

Hold for days to weeks on daily charts. Breakouts, pullbacks to moving averages and patterns are their bread and butter.

Intraday & F&O traders

Work 1 to 15-minute charts with VWAP, CPR, open interest and option-chain data. Highest activity, lowest win rate.

03 Foundations

Chart types, timeframes and scales

The same prices can be drawn many ways. Each type hides some noise and some information.

Chart type switcher

SIM data · last 120 sessions
Type

Line

Connects closing prices only. Cleanest view of trend and long-term levels. Hides intraday range and gaps.

OHLC bar

Vertical line from low to high, tick left for open, tick right for close. Same information as a candle in a quieter form.

Candlestick

Body between open and close, coloured green (close > open) or red. Wicks show extremes. The most popular format in India.

Heikin-Ashi

Averaged candles: HA close = (O+H+L+C)/4, HA open = midpoint of the previous HA body. Long runs of one colour make trends obvious, but HA prices are not tradable prices.

Renko & Point-and-Figure

Time is removed; a new brick or box prints only after price moves a fixed amount (for example 50 Nifty points or 1 ATR). Good for clean support/resistance, useless for timing.

Log vs linear

On a log scale equal percentage moves are equal heights. Use log for any multi-year chart: Nifty going 8,000 to 16,000 (+100%) should look bigger than 16,000 to 24,000 (+50%).

Pick a trading timeframe that matches your holding period, then look one level higher for context and one level lower for entry timing. This "rule of three" is covered in Module 13.

Reading any candle in four questions

  1. Colour: did buyers (close > open) or sellers win?
  2. Body size vs recent bodies: a body twice the average is conviction; a tiny body is indecision.
  3. Wicks: a long lower wick means price was rejected from below (buyers stepped in); a long upper wick means supply.
  4. Location: the same hammer means little mid-range but a lot at a tested support or the 200-day average. Context beats shape.

05 Price structure

Trend, swings and Dow Theory

An uptrend is a series of higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL). A downtrend is lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL). Everything else is a range.

Swing structure detector

Swing = bar whose high (low) is the highest (lowest) of N bars on each side

Dow Theory in six lines

  • Three trends: primary (months to years), secondary corrections (weeks to months, often retracing a third to two-thirds), minor (days).
  • Three phases of a bull market: accumulation by informed money, public participation (the big middle move), and excess when everyone is talking about stocks.
  • Indices must confirm each other. In India, watch Nifty with Nifty Bank: a Nifty high without a Bank Nifty high is a warning.
  • Volume confirms the trend: it should expand in the direction of the trend.
  • A trend persists until a clear reversal signal: in an uptrend, the first lower low after a lower high.
  • Closing prices matter most. A break on closing basis is more reliable than an intraday poke.

Trendlines and channels

Join at least two swing lows in an uptrend (two swing highs in a downtrend); a third touch validates the line. Draw a parallel through the opposite swing to form a channel. Steeper lines break sooner. A break of a trendline signals the trend is slowing, not that it has reversed; reversal needs the swing structure to change.

Nifty exampleFrom the March 2020 low near 7,511, Nifty made a textbook series of higher highs and higher lows through 2020 and 2021, reaching about 18,600 in October 2021 without a single lower low on the weekly chart. Trend followers who simply held while the weekly structure stayed intact captured most of a move of roughly 145%.
06 Price structure

Support, resistance and gaps

Support is a price zone where demand has absorbed selling before. Resistance is where supply has capped rallies. They are zones, not lines.

Automatic S/R zones

Clusters swing points within a tolerance; thicker zones have more touches

Where levels come from

  • Prior swing highs and lows
  • Long consolidation ranges (lots of volume traded there)
  • Round numbers: Nifty 20,000 / 25,000, Bank Nifty 50,000, a stock at ₹1,000
  • Big gaps and the high/low of a big-volume day
  • The 50 and 200-day moving averages (dynamic support)
  • Highest call and put open-interest strikes (Module 15)

Rules of thumb

  • Role reversal: broken resistance tends to become support, and vice versa
  • The more touches, the more significant the level, but also the more likely it eventually breaks
  • Judge breaks on a closing basis, ideally with above-average volume
  • Put stops beyond the zone, not on the line where everyone else's stop sits

07 Price structure

Chart patterns and measured moves

Patterns are pauses in supply and demand that resolve in a direction. Each has a trigger (the breakout), an invalidation (the stop) and a measured target.

Measured-move calculator

Target = breakout level ± pattern height

Default: Nifty's Dec-2022 high and Jun-2022 low, the box Nifty broke out of in mid-2023.

Failed patterns are signals tooA head-and-shoulders that breaks its neckline and then quickly closes back above the right shoulder traps shorts and often fuels a sharp rally. If price reclaims the breakout level within two or three sessions, exit and consider the opposite trade.
08 Indicators

Moving averages

A moving average smooths price to show direction. It lags by design: the longer the period, the smoother and slower it is.

Crossover lab

Triangles mark golden (▲) and death (▼) crosses

SMA (simple) weights every bar equally. EMA (exponential) weights recent bars more, using α = 2/(n+1), so it turns faster. WMA and Hull MA reduce lag further at the cost of noise.

  • Slope tells trend. Rising 50-day EMA and price above it: favour longs.
  • Dynamic support: in strong uptrends, the 20 EMA (fast stocks) or 50 DMA (steady large-caps) often hold pullbacks.
  • Golden cross (50 crosses above 200) and death cross are slow, widely reported signals; good for regime, poor for timing.
  • Common Indian settings: 20 / 50 / 200 DMA on daily charts; 9 / 21 EMA for intraday; the 200 DMA is the line most institutions and media use to call a stock "in a bull or bear phase".
  • Whipsaws: in sideways markets, crossovers flip back and forth and lose money. Use the ADX (Module 10) or a higher-timeframe filter.
09 Indicators

Momentum oscillators: RSI, MACD, Stochastic

Oscillators measure the speed of price. They warn when a move is stretched and when momentum disagrees with price.

RSI with divergence finder

Dashed lines connect bearish (price HH, RSI LH) and bullish divergences

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

RSI = 100 − 100/(1 + RS), where RS is the Wilder-smoothed average gain divided by average loss over n periods (default 14). It runs 0 to 100.

  • Range shift: in bull trends RSI tends to hold 40–80; in bear trends 20–60. RSI failing to get above 60 on rallies is a bearish character change.
  • Overbought is not a sell signal in a strong trend. Strong Indian momentum stocks can stay above 70 for weeks.
  • Divergence: price makes a higher high while RSI makes a lower high (bearish), or the opposite at lows (bullish). Divergence warns; a break of structure confirms.
  • Popular in India: RSI(14) daily above 60 as a momentum filter for swing longs; weekly RSI below 30 in large-caps is rare and has marked major lows (Nifty in March 2020).

MACD and Stochastic

MACD(12,26,9) histogram and Stochastic(14,3,3)

MACD

MACD line = EMA(12) − EMA(26). Signal = EMA(9) of MACD. Histogram = MACD − Signal.

  • Crossovers of the signal line give trade signals; crossovers of zero confirm trend change
  • Shrinking histogram bars show fading momentum before price turns
  • Works best in trending markets; lags in chop

Stochastic

%K = (Close − Lowest low n) / (Highest high n − Lowest low n) × 100, smoothed; %D is its 3-period average.

  • Above 80 overbought, below 20 oversold
  • Best used in ranges, or in the direction of the trend: buy oversold crosses in an uptrend
  • Fast and noisy; many false signals in trends
10 Indicators

Volatility and trend strength: Bollinger, ATR, Supertrend, ADX, VIX

Volatility tells you how far price normally moves. That decides your stop distance, position size and which tools to use.

Bollinger Bands and squeeze

Shaded bars in the lower pane: bandwidth in its lowest 15% of the last 120 bars

Bollinger Bands = 20-period SMA ± 2 standard deviations. About 90% of closes fall inside. Narrow bands (a squeeze) precede big moves; in strong trends price "walks the band". A close outside the band is not by itself a reversal signal.

Supertrend, ATR and ADX

Supertrend is popular among Indian traders; (10, 3) is the common default

ATR

Average True Range: the average of max(H−L, |H−prev C|, |L−prev C|). Gaps count. Use it to size stops: 1.5–2× ATR beyond entry keeps you clear of normal noise.

Supertrend

Bands at (H+L)/2 ± m×ATR that ratchet only in the trend's direction. Green below price = long; flips red on a close through it. Simple trailing stop; whipsaws in ranges.

ADX / DMI

ADX measures trend strength, not direction. Above 25: trending, use trend tools. Below 20: ranging, use oscillators. +DI above −DI favours bulls.

India VIX expected-move calculator

1σ range ≈ 68% probability; uses VIX ÷ √(365/days)

Typical calm-market VIX is 11–15; above 20 is stressed; March 2020 saw it above 80.

Price tells you what happened. Volume tells you how much conviction was behind it.

OBV

Adds the day's volume on up closes and subtracts it on down closes. An OBV breakout ahead of price suggests accumulation.

VWAP

Volume-weighted average price from the open. Intraday institutions benchmark against it: above VWAP, buyers are in control. See Module 16.

Delivery % (India-specific)

NSE publishes how much of each day's volume was taken into delivery rather than squared off intraday. A price rise on high volume and rising delivery % points to genuine buying rather than speculative churn.

13 Levels & advanced

Fibonacci, pivot points and CPR

Calculated levels give everyone the same reference prices. That shared attention is why they sometimes work.

Fibonacci retracement & extension

Default: Nifty's Sep-2024 peak and Apr-2025 low (approximate)
Last move was

Retracements measure how much of the last swing has been given back. In healthy trends pullbacks often stall between 38.2% and 61.8%; the 50% level is not a Fibonacci ratio but is watched just as much. A retracement beyond 78.6% usually means the prior move has failed. Extensions (127.2%, 161.8%) project targets once price breaks the swing extreme.

Pivot points & Central Pivot Range

Enter the previous session's high, low and close

CPR is a favourite of Indian intraday traders. It has three lines: the pivot P = (H+L+C)/3, the bottom central level BC = (H+L)/2 and the top central level TC = 2P − BC. A narrow CPR after a quiet day often precedes a trending day; a wide CPR suggests a range day. Opening above the CPR is bullish bias, below it bearish. Use daily CPR for intraday, weekly CPR for swing trades.

Ichimoku, multiple timeframes, breadth and relative strength

Once the basics are second nature, these tools add context: the bigger trend, the health of the whole market and where money is rotating.

Daily vs weekly: the same market at two timeframes

Weekly bars are built from the daily bars on the left

Weekly · same data, ~2 years
Look for daily pullbacks that are just higher lows on the weekly chart.

Multiple-timeframe analysis

  1. Define the trend on the higher timeframe (weekly for swing trades). Only trade in its direction.
  2. Find the setup on the trading timeframe (daily): a pullback to support, a flag, a squeeze.
  3. Time the entry on the lower timeframe (hourly): a break of the pullback's minor downtrend line.

Ichimoku Kinko Hyo

Tenkan 9, Kijun 26, Senkou B 52; cloud shifted 26 bars forward

Ichimoku in one minute

  • Price above the cloud: uptrend. Inside: no trade. Below: downtrend
  • Tenkan crossing above Kijun: bullish trigger, stronger above the cloud
  • A thick cloud is strong support or resistance; a thin cloud breaks easily
  • Chikou (close plotted 26 bars back) above past price confirms

Elliott Wave in one minute

  • Trends move in 5 waves (1-2-3-4-5), corrections in 3 (A-B-C)
  • Wave 3 is never the shortest; wave 4 should not overlap wave 1
  • Wave 2 often retraces 50–61.8% of wave 1
  • Useful as a map; highly subjective as a trigger. Two analysts rarely count the same chart the same way

Market breadth

  • Advance/decline ratio on NSE: above 1.5 broad buying, below 0.7 broad selling
  • % of Nifty 500 above 200 DMA: above 70% is strong but stretched; below 20% is washed out
  • Nifty rising while breadth falls means a few heavyweights carry the index; fragile

Relative strength & rotation

  • Plot stock ÷ Nifty. A rising ratio means it is outperforming, even if both fall
  • Leaders make new RS highs before price highs
  • Rotation: money moves between sectors (IT to PSU banks to defence to auto). Chart sector indices against Nifty weekly
  • Intermarket: crude oil up hurts OMCs and paints; a weaker rupee helps IT exporters; FII selling weighs on banks

Reading derivatives data: open interest, PCR and max pain

India has one of the world's most active options markets. Positioning data from the option chain adds a layer that price charts alone can't show.

Option chain: PCR and max pain

Put-Call Ratio

Total put OI ÷ total call OI. Around 0.7–1.2 is normal for Nifty. Very high (above 1.4) means heavy put writing, often bullish support but also complacency. Very low (below 0.6) means bearish positioning. Use it as a contrarian gauge at extremes only.

OI walls

The strike with the most call OI acts as near-term resistance because call writers defend it; the biggest put-OI strike acts as support. Watch for shifts: put writers moving up to higher strikes is bullish.

Max pain

The expiry price at which option buyers collectively lose the most (and writers pay out the least). Prices sometimes gravitate there on expiry day. A weak tendency, not a magnet.

Read SEBI's numbers before trading optionsSEBI's September 2024 study found 93% of individual F&O traders lost money between FY22 and FY24, with aggregate losses above ₹1.8 lakh crore. Use OI data to understand the market even if you only trade cash equities.
16 Levels & advanced

The intraday toolkit: VWAP, opening range and CPR

Intraday charts have their own rhythm in India: a volatile first 30 minutes, a quieter midday, and a pickup into the 15:00–15:30 window that sets the official close.

SIM 5-minute session, 9:15 to 15:30

75 five-minute candles; VWAP, 15-min opening range and CPR from the previous day

Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

  • Mark the high and low of the first 15 minutes (9:15–9:30)
  • Go long on a 5-min close above the high, short below the low
  • Stop at the opposite side or mid-range; target 1–2× the range
  • Skip it on very wide opening ranges or narrow-CPR conflicts

VWAP rules

  • Above a rising VWAP: buy pullbacks to VWAP
  • Below a falling VWAP: sell rallies to it
  • Repeated crossings means a range day; stand aside or fade extremes
  • Anchored VWAP from a results day or a big gap acts as a swing level

Gap strategies

  • Gap and go: gap above yesterday's high that holds the first 15-min low
  • Gap fill: gap into resistance that fails to make a new high in 30 minutes
  • Use GIFT Nifty before 9:00 to anticipate the gap

Intraday discipline

  • Brokers auto square-off MIS positions near 15:15–15:20
  • Cap the number of trades and a daily loss limit (for example 2% of capital)
  • Avoid the first 5 minutes unless trading the open deliberately
  • Costs (brokerage, STT, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty) matter far more at intraday scale
17 Trading it

Risk management and position sizing

Your edge decides whether you make money. Your position size decides whether you survive long enough for the edge to show.

Position-size calculator

Fixed-fractional: risk a set % of capital per trade
Stop

Expectancy & Monte Carlo

50 random sequences of trades with your win rate and payoff

Rules that keep traders in the game

  • Risk 0.5–2% of capital per trade. At 1%, ten straight losses cost about 10%. At 10%, they cost 65%.
  • Losses compound against you: a 50% drawdown needs a 100% gain to recover.
  • Decide the stop before the entry. Size from the stop, never the other way round.
  • Aim for reward:risk of at least 1.5–2. With 2R winners you only need to be right 34% of the time to break even before costs.
  • Correlation is hidden size: five long positions in private banks are one big bet on Bank Nifty.
  • Gap risk: stops do not protect against overnight gaps or circuits. Size smaller into results and events.
  • Leverage in F&O magnifies everything. One Nifty futures lot controls several lakh rupees of exposure.
18 Trading it

Strategy backtester

Test classic rules on simulated markets. Generate several random markets: a rule that only works on one is not a strategy.

Long-only backtest · signals on close, fills at next open

Backtesting traps
  • Overfitting: tuning parameters until the past looks perfect. Test on data you didn't tune on.
  • Look-ahead bias: using today's close to trade at today's close. This tester fills at the next open.
  • Survivorship bias: testing on today's Nifty 50 ignores stocks that were dropped (Yes Bank, for example).
  • Ignoring costs and slippage: STT, brokerage and impact cost can erase a high-frequency edge.
  • Too few trades: fewer than 30–50 trades proves nothing.

20 Trading it

Psychology, common mistakes and your trading plan

Most chart knowledge fails at the moment of execution. Write the rules down while calm, then follow them when you're not.

Mistakes that cost Indian retail traders most

  • Buying out-of-the-money options on expiry day as lottery tickets
  • Averaging down a losing trade ("it's a good company")
  • Moving the stop-loss further away once hit
  • Following Telegram tips and "operator" stocks in illiquid small-caps
  • Over-trading after a loss to "make it back" (revenge trading)
  • Ignoring the broader trend: buying breakouts while Nifty is below its 200 DMA
  • Ten indicators on one chart that all say the same thing

Biases to watch

  • Confirmation bias: only seeing the signals that agree with your position
  • Loss aversion: cutting winners early, holding losers
  • Recency bias: assuming the last month's trend is permanent
  • Anchoring: "it was ₹1,500 last year, so ₹900 is cheap"
  • Overconfidence after a winning streak: size creeps up just before the drawdown
