    LLM Wiki · equity research edition

    Build a research wiki on your company that Claude keeps up to date

    A step-by-step setup for Andrej Karpathy's LLM Wiki idea, adapted for following one listed company. You collect filings, transcripts and news. Claude reads them and writes a linked set of notes. You read and explore those notes in Obsidian.

    Time: about 2 hours for setup, then 10–20 min per document Cost: a paid Claude plan (Pro or Max); Obsidian is free Skill needed: none, every click is explained

    00

    Understand the idea before you click anything

    10 minutes of reading

    When you normally ask an AI about a company, it reads your documents from scratch every time and forgets everything afterwards. Ask the same question next month and it starts again from zero.

    Karpathy's LLM Wiki flips this around. Each time you add a document, Claude reads it once and writes what it learned into a set of linked notes: a page on the business model, a page per competitor, a running timeline, a tracker of what management promised. The next document updates those same pages. Over time the notes get richer instead of being thrown away. In his words, the wiki is "a persistent, compounding artifact."

    For equity research this is a natural fit. Analysts already keep a company file. The tedious part is keeping it current: updating the numbers after each quarter, noting where management changed its story, linking a new risk to the old ones. That bookkeeping is the part Claude does here.

    Layer 3 · the rulebook

    CLAUDE.md

    The three things you'll ask Claude to do

    Ingest

    "Read this new document and update the wiki." One source can touch 10–15 pages.

    Query

    "What did management say about margins over the last year?" Claude answers from the wiki, with citations. Good answers get saved as new pages.

    Lint

    "Check the wiki's health." Claude looks for contradictions, stale numbers, missing links and gaps.

    01

    Get Claude ready on your computer

    15 minutes
    In plain English

    The normal Claude chat website can't open or save files on your computer, so it can't maintain a wiki. You need a version of Claude that can work inside a folder on your machine. That version is called Claude Code. Despite the name, you won't write any code. It is Claude with permission to read and write files in one folder you choose.

    1. Make sure you have a paid Claude plan. Go to claude.ai/upgrade. Pro is enough to start; long annual reports use a lot of your allowance, so consider Max if you run out often.
    2. Download the Claude desktop app from claude.ai/download, install it, and sign in with the same account.
    3. Inside the app, look for the Code tab. That's where you'll point Claude at your wiki folder in Step 6. You don't need to use it yet.
    If you can't find a Code tab

    App layouts change. Step 6 also shows the terminal method, which works on every computer. It looks intimidating but is three typed lines.

    02

    Install Obsidian and create your vault

    10 minutes
    In plain English

    Obsidian is a free note-taking app that works on plain text files stored on your computer. A vault is just a normal folder that Obsidian watches. Because the notes are ordinary files, Claude can write them and Obsidian shows them the moment they change. Karpathy's summary: "Obsidian is the IDE; the LLM is the programmer; the wiki is the codebase."

    1. Download Obsidian from obsidian.md/download and install it.
    2. Open it and choose Create new vault.
    3. Vault name: EquityWiki
    4. Location: pick your Documents folder. Your vault will live at . Write this path down; you'll need it in Step 6.
    5. Click Create. You'll see an empty window. That's correct.
    Avoid

    Don't put the vault inside iCloud Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox for now. Sync services sometimes lock files while Claude is writing them. You can add backup later (Step 12).

    03

    Change four Obsidian settings

    10 minutes
    In plain English

    These settings make Obsidian store files where Claude expects them and use the same link style Claude will write. Settings is the gear icon in the bottom-left corner.

    1. Settings → Files and links → Default location for new attachments: choose "In the folder specified below" and type raw/assets. Why: any image you paste or download lands in one predictable place Claude can look in.
    2. Settings → Files and links → Use [[Wikilinks]]: turn it on. Why: Claude will link pages as [[business-model]]; this makes Obsidian treat those as clickable links.
    3. Settings → Files and links → Automatically update internal links: turn it on. Why: if a page is renamed, links to it keep working.
    4. Settings → Hotkeys: search for Download attachments and assign Cmd + Shift + D. Why: when you clip a news article that contains charts, one key press saves the images locally so Claude can actually see them.

    Two built-in views you'll use constantly: Graph view (Cmd + G) draws every page as a dot with lines for links, and Backlinks (right sidebar) lists every page that mentions the one you're reading.

    04

    Know what goes where

    5 minutes · Claude will create these for you in Step 7
    In plain English

    There are two zones. raw/ is your filing cabinet: original documents, never changed. wiki/ is Claude's notebook: everything Claude writes. Keeping them apart means you can always check a note against the original. Click any folder to see what belongs in it.

      05

      Create the rulebook: CLAUDE.md

      10 minutes · the most important step
      In plain English

      Claude Code automatically reads a file named CLAUDE.md in the folder it works in, every time. Whatever you write there becomes standing instructions. This is how Claude knows to cite page numbers, keep facts apart from opinion, and track management guidance, without you repeating it. Karpathy calls this the schema, and says it's what turns Claude from a generic chatbot into a disciplined wiki maintainer.

      1. In Obsidian, right-click the vault name at the top of the left sidebar (or the empty file list) and choose New note.
      2. Name it exactly CLAUDE. Obsidian adds .md itself. It must sit at the top level of the vault, not inside a folder.
      3. Click Copy on the box below and paste it into the note. It is already filled in with your company.
      CLAUDE.md
      
      
      
        
      What the important rules do
      
        
        
          
      • raw/ is read-only. Your originals can never be damaged, so every claim stays checkable.
        • 
          
      • Cite source and page for every number. When a figure looks wrong, you click the link and check.
        • 
          
      • "What the company reports" vs "Our read". Facts and interpretation never blur together.
        • 
          
      • Flag contradictions instead of overwriting. When Q3 says something different from Q1, that change is often the most useful finding.
        • 
          
      • Guidance tracker. Every promise management makes gets a row with a deadline. Over a few quarters this shows you how reliable they are.
        • 
          
      • Wait for my OK on ingest. You see the takeaways before anything is written, so you stay in charge of what matters.
        • 
        
      
        
      You'll edit this file over time. If Claude keeps doing something you dislike, add a rule. That's the intended way to improve the wiki.

      09
      Ingest your first document
      20–30 minutes for an annual report
      
      
      In plain English
      "Ingest" means Claude reads one document and folds what it learned into the wiki. Karpathy recommends doing them one at a time while you stay involved: read the summary, tell Claude what to emphasise, then let it update the pages.
      
      
      Order matters
      
      
        
        
      1. Annual report first. It builds the foundation: business model, segments, management, risks.
        2. 
        
      2. Then quarterly material, oldest to newest: results, presentation and transcript for each quarter. Going forward in time lets the timeline and guidance tracker build naturally, and lets Claude spot when the story changes.
        3. 
        
      3. Rating report and news last. They add colour on top of the core.
        4. 
      
      
      
      Paste into Claude · edit the file name
      
      
      What happens next
      
      
        
        
      1. Claude reads the report in sections. A 300-page annual report takes a while; let it work.
        2. 
        
      2. It lists the key takeaways and waits. This is your moment: reply with anything like "Focus more on the retail segment" or "The debt refinancing is the key point."
        3. 
        
      3. Reply OK, go ahead. Claude writes the source page, updates the company pages, fills the guidance tracker, and updates the index and log.
        4.

        5. 
      
      
      
      Check the work
      Pick three numbers from the new pages and verify them against the PDF using the cited page numbers. If one is wrong, tell Claude and ask it to fix it. Do this for every early ingest until you trust the pattern.

      Aa
      Words you'll meet
      
      
      
        
      Markdown (.md)
      Plain text with light formatting: # for headings, - for bullets. Readable by people, apps and AI.
      
        
      Vault
      The folder Obsidian treats as your notebook.
      
        
      Wikilink
      [[page-name]], a clickable link from one note to another.
      
        
      Frontmatter
      The small block between --- lines at the top of a page, holding facts like type and date.
      
        
      Schema
      Karpathy's word for the rulebook. Here that's CLAUDE.md.
      
        
      Ingest
      Claude reading one source and updating the wiki with it.
      
        
      Lint
      A health check for contradictions, gaps and broken links.
      
        
      RAG
      The usual approach, where AI searches raw documents fresh for every question. The wiki replaces that with notes built once and kept current.
      
        
      Terminal
      A text window for typing commands to your computer.
      
        
      Orphan page
      A page nothing links to. Lint finds these.
